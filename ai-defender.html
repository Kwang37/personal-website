01 / Context

The risk wasn’t just phishing. It was disruption.

For healthcare workers, malicious emails can expose sensitive patient information. But mistakenly blocking a legitimate clinical message can also interrupt important work.

My resume describes product leadership on the four-person capstone team and eight stakeholder interviews with clinicians and healthcare IT professionals. The public repository documents two interview-note links, but does not independently confirm the complete count. The team built a synthetic dentistry-clinic scenario to explore phishing, social engineering, potential PHI exposure and alert fatigue without handling real patient data. The central product question was: how might we automate triage without giving up human judgment?

Stakeholder interview documentation in README

Design principle: Quarantine suspicious emails for review instead of permanently deleting messages based on a probabilistic model output.

02 / Product decisions

Safety by design, not safety as a setting.

01
Quarantine-first

Hold potentially suspicious messages instead of removing them outright, preserving the possibility of human correction.

02
Separate roles

Staff see relevant inbox views; IT reviewers can inspect quarantine decisions and logs. Front-end authorization in the prototype is simulated.

03
Human-in-the-loop

Reviewer feedback and corrections inform subsequent classification workflow in the team's experimental system.

04
Test without PHI

Generate synthetic MIME messages and simulated healthcare roles, keeping actual patient data out of the experiment.

04 / System architecture

From a generated email to a reviewable decision.

The broader team system uses scheduled synthetic email delivery, a controller/classification workflow and AWS services to produce traceable outcomes for reviewers. The repository describes Lambda, S3, EventBridge and CloudWatch monitoring; server.js also references DynamoDB for review and feedback-related data.

07 / Reflection

A prototype is not a clinical product.

The architecture makes review possible, but a production healthcare deployment would require much more than a working demo. The README records important limitations: inconsistent confidence values, developing caching strategies and synthetic emails that are not fully representative of real-world conditions.

What worked

Quarantine rather than delete; role-specific review surfaces; visible CloudWatch evidence; testing without real patient information.

What I would improve

Calibrate confidence scores, track false positive/negative rates, strengthen access control, and run realistic testing with healthcare security review before real deployment.

Scope note: This student team project is a synthetic cybersecurity experiment, not a HIPAA-certified clinical deployment. Team system architecture and outcomes are separated from my confirmed HTML/AWS contribution.
