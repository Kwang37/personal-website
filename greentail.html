01 / The opportunity

Buying “sustainable” pet food shouldn’t require a research degree.

The project tackles a familiar consumer dilemma: pet owners want food that is appropriate for their animals, affordable and environmentally responsible, but product claims are difficult to compare.

The team used two personas: Emma, an experienced, sustainability-conscious pet owner who values transparent sourcing and privacy; and Sarah, a new pet parent who needs approachable nutrition guidance. A useful experience needed to support both research-heavy comparisons and guided first-time discovery.

Emma / Transparent decisions

Compare ingredients, sustainability indicators and value without being forced to register.

Sarah / Guided learning

Answer a short set of questions, understand why products match, and access approachable feeding education.

04 / Privacy as a product decision

Let users explore without asking who they are.

I advocated using browser-local storage and making account creation optional rather than a gateway to core features.

The repository implements quiz state with React Context. Searching, taking the quiz and comparing products do not require authentication. Saved items and prototype profile information use localStorage. This reduces the need to transmit basic browsing preferences to a central service during core discovery, but it is not equivalent to encrypted or inherently secure storage.

In a real deployment I would separate anonymous preferences from secure server-managed authentication, review retention and consent, and avoid storing passwords client-side.

05 / Product details

Make environmental trade-offs easier to understand.

Match with understandable criteria

The repository’s weighted matcher considers pet type, life stage, weight, ingredients to avoid, budget, eco features and feeding style. The code limits output to the top eight ranked products.

Compare beyond a single badge

The compare architecture calls for side-by-side nutrition, ingredients, price, best-fit guidance and clear follow-up actions rather than opaque “best” claims.

Maps with honesty about data

The React-Leaflet modal filters modeled stores by ZIP and selected product/brand, but the store database is a demonstration dataset rather than real-time stock.

Context for beginners

The educational experience embeds video and guidance on labels, organic terms and feeding transitions, providing help without leaving users stuck at the choice stage.

07 / Reflection

Good UX makes trade-offs visible.

The strongest outcome of this project is a functioning, connected React prototype that a user can actually explore, not an invented conversion metric. For me, the important learning was balancing four competing priorities: clear sustainability information, relevant nutrition, low-friction workflows and careful data handling.

What shipped in the team prototype

Multi-step onboarding, results, search and comparison, sample store maps, education and browser-local demo state.

What comes next

Validate sustainability data provenance, add usability study results, replace mock authentication, and use verified inventory data if the map is ever positioned as live availability.
